### Most perfume is an invention. Ours is a record: cold air, old larch and living stone, taken from the mountain and held still.

AURELLE works from a small laboratory in Chamonix, under the Aiguille du Midi. The botanicals are gathered by guides of the Compagnie des Guides de Chamonix in the brief alpine summer, under the quotas of the Mont-Blanc massif, and never below the roots.

Conventional distillation boils what is delicate. We freeze it instead. Every flacon is numbered; the house makes three hundred and fifty a year and no more. What the mountain does not give, we do not sell.

## Section #method: Cryo-enfleurage
### Cryo-enfleurage

Steam destroys the top of a mountain plant: the volatile, cold-smelling molecules go first. So nothing in this house is boiled. It is frozen, drawn under vacuum, and left alone in the dark.

-

#### Gathered above the treeline

Edelweiss, génépi, larch and fir, cut by certified guides in the few weeks the massif allows, with the roots left in place.

HarvestAbove 2,200 m

-

#### Frozen within two hours

Flash-frozen on the mountain so the fragile terpene chains that normally die in transport arrive in the laboratory intact.

Chamber−24 °C, under vacuum

-

#### Balanced with meltwater

Each formula is finished with glacial meltwater filtered for centuries through Mont-Blanc granite, on an organic French wheat alcohol base.

Base85 % vol, organic

-

#### Ninety days in darkness

The extrait rests in cold-tempered steel in the cellar before it is numbered by hand and sealed.

Edition350 flacons a year

## A perfumer who was a guide first

Origin

### The roof of Europe

Haute-Savoie does not produce an ornamental fantasy. It produces an uncompromising olfactory truth: air without pollution, ozone chilled on glacier tongues, resilient flora clinging to sheer granite. Jean-Marc Faure spent twenty seasons guiding on the massif before he tried to keep any of it in a bottle.

Method

### Why nothing is boiled

Conventional distillation destroys what makes a mountain plant smell of the mountain. The laboratory in Chamonix was built around a single idea: freeze the harvest within two hours, extract it under vacuum at minus twenty-four degrees, and finish it with meltwater filtered through granite. The house calls this cryo-enfleurage.

The result is three extraits, each from one band of altitude. Treeline at 1,650 metres, Sommet at 2,042, Crevasse at 3,100.

Harvest

### With the Compagnie des Guides

Edelweiss and wild génépi are gathered above 2,200 metres by certified alpinists of the Compagnie des Guides de Chamonix, under the quotas of the massif. The protocol leaves root systems untouched so that what is cut this year grows back the next. The house works with roughly a hundred and twenty kilograms of raw material a year, and no more.

Edition

### Three hundred and fifty

Each year the house numbers three hundred and fifty flacons. When they are gone, the year is closed. A heavy glass flacon with a basalt stopper, a frosted crystal with titanium, an amber apothecary bottle with charred larch: each is made to be kept and refilled, not thrown away.

“We do not blend to mask reality. We capture the clarity of high-altitude wind, ancient larch forests and living stone.”Jean-Marc Faure, maître parfumeur and mountain guide
