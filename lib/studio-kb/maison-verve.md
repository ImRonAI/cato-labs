## The hours
no one sees.

Maison Verve builds four calibres and three pieces a year at Quai des Bergues. Every bridge, wheel and screw is finished by hand, then hidden beneath the dial. Scroll, and the movement opens.

Open the movement
Speak with the atelier

I · Twin barrels

### Wound once.
Ninety-six hours.

Two mainsprings, coupled in series, carry Calibre MV-09 through four days away from the wrist. The barrel turns once in seven hours; nothing in the movement is in a hurry.

- **Power reserve**: 96 hours

- **Barrels**: Two

II · The train

### Sixty to one.

Centre wheel to third pinion, eighty teeth into ten. Third to fourth, seventy-five into ten. The hour becomes the minute. Fourth wheel to escape pinion, seventy-two into six, and the minute becomes twelve turns of the escape wheel.

- **Reduction**: 60 : 1

- **Escape wheel**: 15 teeth

III · The balance

### Three beats
a second.

Twenty-one thousand six hundred vibrations an hour, inside a carriage that completes one turn each minute. Each calibre is adjusted in six positions before it leaves the bench.

- **Frequency**: 21,600 vph

- **Adjusted**: 6 positions

IV · The finishing

### Twelve operations
before it is seen.

Geneva stripes drawn across the bridges, perlage on the plate, every edge bevelled and the steel brought to a black polish. Then the dial goes on, and the work disappears. The Poinçon de Genève attests it was done.

- **Components**: 342

- **Jewels**: 38

## Section #finishing: Work that is
meant to be hidden.
Finishing

### Work that is
meant to be hidden.

Up to twelve operations pass over each component before it is assembled. None of them make the watch run better. All of them are required by the Poinçon de Genève, and by us.

-
01

#### AnglageHand-bevelled edges

Every edge of every bridge is filed to a forty-five degree bevel, then polished with diamond paste on a stick of gentian wood until the facet holds a single line of light. Inward angles cannot be reached by a machine. They are cut by hand, or not at all.

[image: Hand-bevelled anglage bridge macro on Calibre MV-09]

-
02

#### Côtes de GenèveGeneva stripes

Parallel waves drawn across the upper surfaces of the bridges with a rotating boxwood disc. Each stripe is one pass, each pass by hand, so the pattern reads continuously from one bridge to the next across the whole movement.

[image: Côtes de Genève stripes across Maison Verve movement bridges]

-
03

#### PerlageCircular graining

Overlapping circles stamped into the mainplate with a spinning peg, one at a time, in an even row. The plate is never visible once the watch is cased. It is finished anyway.

[image: Circular perlage graining on Maison Verve mainplate]

-
04

#### Black polishPoli noir on steel

A flat steel surface lapped on tin until it is so true that it reflects light in one direction only. Seen straight on, it is pitch black. Tilted, it is a mirror. The tourbillon bridge of the MV-01 and the hammers of the MV-03 are finished this way, as are the heads of the blued screws.

[image: Black-polished steel tourbillon bridge and blued screws]

## Section #maison: An independent house on the Quai des Bergues, making watches the slow way because there is no other way to make them.
The maison

### An independent house on the Quai des Bergues, making watches the slow way because there is no other way to make them.

Maison Verve is a Geneva atelier. We design our own calibres, cut our own wheels, and finish every component by hand in the same rooms where the watches are assembled and regulated. Nothing is sent out.

Each calibre submits to the Poinçon de Genève, the city's own seal, which inspects the finishing of the movement rather than its performance. The performance we test ourselves: every watch is adjusted in six positions and timed before it is cased.

We make three references a year, in editions of eight, twelve and fifteen. The flagship Calibre MV-09, a tourbillon chronometer with twin barrels, is made to order and is never produced in series.

- **Atelier**: Quai des Bergues, Geneva

- **Certification**: Poinçon de Genève

- **Annual output**: Thirty-five pieces

- **Calibres**: Four, in-house

## Tourbillon Souverain

The quietest watch we make. A flying tourbillon turns once a minute in an aperture at six, beneath a dial of Grand Feu enamel fired white, in a platinum case of 41.5 millimetres.

- **Case**: Platinum 950, 41.5 mm

- **Dial**: Grand Feu white enamel

- **Movement**: Calibre MV-01, hand-wound

- **Complication**: Flying tourbillon, zero-reset seconds

- **Frequency**: 21,600 vph (3 Hz)

- **Power reserve**: 72 hours

- **Jewels**: 31

- **Daily rate**: −1 / +2 seconds

- **Certification**: Poinçon de Genève

- **Edition**: 8 pieces

### The movement

The tourbillon is flying: held from below only, so nothing crosses the carriage and the balance is seen whole. Pull the crown and the seconds return to zero, the carriage stopping so the watch can be set to the second.

### The dial

Enamel is fired at around eight hundred degrees, several times, and each firing can crack it. A dial that survives is white in a way paint never is. The numerals are fired into it, not printed on.

### The case

Platinum 950, polished and brushed by hand. The tourbillon bridge beneath is steel brought to a black polish; from most angles it reads as a void.

MV-02

## Quantième Perpétuel

A calendar that knows the length of every month and the leap of every fourth year, in a rose gold case of 40 millimetres. The moon is engraved by hand in white gold and set against aventurine glass.

- **Case**: 18k rose gold, 40 mm

- **Dial**: Opaline silver, aventurine moon disc

- **Movement**: Calibre MV-02, self-winding

- **Complication**: Perpetual calendar, moon phase

- **Frequency**: 28,800 vph (4 Hz)

- **Power reserve**: 60 hours

- **Jewels**: 45

- **Daily rate**: −0 / +3 seconds

- **Certification**: Poinçon de Genève

- **Edition**: 12 pieces

### The movement

A perpetual calendar carries a mechanical memory of the Gregorian cycle: a cam that turns once every four years and tells the date wheel when February has twenty-nine days. Set correctly, it will not need correction until the year 2100.

### The moon

The moon disc is white gold, engraved by hand with a single graver, and set on a disc of aventurine glass whose copper inclusions read as a night sky. It drifts one day in a little over a century.

### The case

Rose gold, 40 millimetres, with a sapphire back that shows the rotor and the stripes beneath it.

## Chronographe Monopoussoir

One pusher starts, stops and returns the chronograph, in that order, through a column wheel and a vertical clutch. Titanium case, 42 millimetres, and the fastest beat in the atelier.

- **Case**: Grade 5 titanium, 42 mm

- **Dial**: Anthracite, two registers

- **Movement**: Calibre MV-03, hand-wound

- **Complication**: Monopusher chronograph, column wheel, vertical clutch

- **Frequency**: 36,000 vph (5 Hz)

- **Power reserve**: 50 hours

- **Jewels**: 52

- **Daily rate**: −1 / +1.5 seconds

- **Certification**: Poinçon de Genève

- **Edition**: 15 pieces

### The movement

The column wheel is the oldest and most exacting way to sequence a chronograph. Its teeth are cut, then each is bevelled by hand. The vertical clutch engages by friction rather than by gear, so the seconds hand starts without a jump.

### The pusher

One pusher, in the crown. Press once to start, once to stop, once to reset. The hammers that return the hands are black-polished steel.

### The case

Titanium is light and hard and difficult to finish well. Ours is brushed on the flanks and polished on the bevels, so the case shows the same two surfaces as the bridges inside it.

## Calibre MV-09

The calibre the atelier is built around. A sixty-second tourbillon driven by two barrels in series, adjusted in six positions and timed to within two seconds a day. It is made only to order, one at a time.

- **Movement**: Calibre MV-09, hand-wound

- **Complication**: 60-second tourbillon

- **Barrels**: Two, in series

- **Frequency**: 21,600 vph (3 Hz)

- **Power reserve**: 96 hours

- **Jewels**: 38

- **Components**: 342

- **Adjustment**: Six positions

- **Daily rate**: 0 / +2 seconds

- **Certification**: Poinçon de Genève

### The movement

Each of the three hundred and forty-two components is finished before it is assembled, and most are finished again after a first assembly reveals where they touch. The train runs sixty to one from centre wheel to carriage.

### The finishing

Côtes de Genève across the bridges, perlage on the plate, every edge bevelled by hand, every screw blued and black-polished. The movement at the top of this page is a model of this calibre, exploded so the layers can be read.

### The commission

There is no series. A client chooses case metal, dial and the hand treatment with the atelier, and the watch is made over the following months. Ask the atelier, or request an hour in Geneva.
