## Section #firm: Most of private capital is waiting. We are good at it. We lend against cash flows we can see, hold through cycles we cannot predict, and let the quantitative work happen quietly in the background, where it belongs.
The firm

### Most of private capital is waiting. We are good at it. We lend against cash flows we can see, hold through cycles we cannot predict, and let the quantitative work happen quietly in the background, where it belongs.

Lend first. Senior, secured, floating-rate loans to middle-market companies in Europe and North America with recurring cash flows. Seniority is the position; yield is the consequence.

Measure always. A quantitative risk function sits beside every mandate: volatility triggers, loan-to-value rebalancing, sovereign-spread monitoring. It informs the committee. It does not replace it.

Say little. We publish partner letters and regulatory filings, and almost nothing else. Clients hear from us when there is something worth saying.

## Section #approach: How a loan is made here.
Approach

### How a loan is made here.

The process is slow by design. Most of what we are shown, we decline. What we keep is held with the attention of a family that intends to be here in thirty years. Our principles set out why.

I · OriginationFewer rooms, better rooms.
Sponsors, advisers and families who know us bring situations that suit us. We do not run auctions and we rarely join them. The best credit arrives by introduction.

II · UnderwritingCash flow, then collateral, then people.
Each underwriting team builds the case from recurring cash flow outward. Loan-to-value is set conservatively and defended in committee. Covenants are written to be used.

III · Quantitative overlayMachines watch. People decide.
A quantitative risk function monitors volatility, spreads and loan-to-value across every mandate in near real time, and raises its hand early. The committee answers.

IV · StewardshipHold, and keep holding.
Positions are held through cycles with regular reporting to clients and regulators, and a partner letter when there is something to say. Liquidity terms are set by the mandate, and kept.

Principles

### Six things we will not change.

Written for new colleagues and shown to new clients. They are not aspirations. They are the reasons we decline most of what we are offered.

01Seniority is a position, yield is a consequence.
We begin every structure by asking where we sit when things go wrong. The coupon follows from that answer, not the other way round.

02Cash flow before collateral before story.
We lend against money a business already makes. Collateral is the second conversation. The plan is the third, and it is never the reason.

03Machines see everything and decide nothing.
Our quantitative function watches every mandate continuously and raises its hand early. The investment committee answers. That order will not change.

04Decline most things.
A small number of good positions, held with attention, is the whole business. The rest is politeness.

05Liquidity terms are promises.
Each mandate sets its own terms, from daily to multi-year, and keeps them. We do not gate, and we do not surprise.

06Say little, and mean it.
We publish letters and filings and almost nothing else. Clients hear from us when there is something worth saying. This page is as loud as we get.

#### Who we lend to

Established businesses with recurring, contracted or highly visible revenue: software with retained customers, services with long contracts, essential industrials. We prefer boring. We prefer owners and sponsors who have been through a downturn and remember it.

#### How the loan is built

Seniority first. Floating rate, so our clients are not writing an interest-rate view into a credit decision. Covenants that mean something and are tested. Loan-to-value is set conservatively and defended before the investment committee, not after.

#### What the machines do

Our quantitative risk function monitors each borrower’s metrics and the wider market for volatility and spread moves, and flags drift early. It gives the committee time. It does not make the decision.

#### What clients should expect

A portfolio of senior loans held to maturity, reported regularly and without ornament. Liquidity terms are set by the mandate. Private credit is illiquid and can lose money; the offering documents describe those risks in full.

Strategy 02

### Mezzanine Capital

Subordinated instruments with equity co-investment rights, for sponsor-backed recapitalisations and growth that is deliberate rather than hopeful.

Position

Subordinated, with equity rights

Use of capital

Recapitalisations, strategic growth

Partners

Private equity sponsors, founder-owners

Loan-to-value

Higher than senior, bounded in committee

Liquidity

Multi-year commitment

Oversight

Quantitative risk overlay

#### Where mezzanine belongs

Between the senior lender and the owner, when a business has outgrown its balance sheet but not its discipline. We take more risk than our direct lending mandate and we are paid for it in coupon and in participation.

#### How we structure it

Subordinated debt with warrants or co-investment rights, sized so that the sponsor still has the most to lose. We prefer to be the only mezzanine lender in the structure and to sit close to the board.

#### How we underwrite it

The same cash-flow-first method as our senior book, with more weight given to the sponsor’s record and to downside cases in which growth does not arrive. If the loan only works when the plan works, we decline.

#### What clients should expect

A smaller, more concentrated book of positions held for several years, with reporting that is candid about what has gone to plan and what has not. Subordinated credit carries a real risk of loss; the offering documents describe it.

Strategy 03

### Special Situations

Liquidity for asset-heavy enterprises in transition: balance sheets being rebuilt, assets being repositioned, time being bought by owners who still have a plan.

Position

Secured against identifiable assets

Situations

Balance-sheet transition, restructuring

Borrowers

Asset-heavy enterprises

Loan-to-value

Set by collateral we can value

Liquidity

Locked for the term of the mandate

Oversight

Quantitative risk overlay

#### The situations we take

Companies with real assets and a temporary problem: a maturity wall, a seller who has run out of patience, a transition that the senior lenders will not fund. We are not a distressed-for-control investor. We lend, and we want to be repaid.

#### How we value the collateral

Conservatively, with our own appraisals and a view on how long it would take to sell in a poor market. Loan-to-value is set against that figure, not the owner’s. Timelines are written into the documents and held to.

#### Why patience matters here

Transitions take longer than anyone’s model. We size positions and set terms so that we can wait, and so that our clients are not asked to.

#### What clients should expect

An opportunistic book with lumpy timing and higher dispersion of outcomes than our senior mandates. Positions are illiquid and may lose money; eligibility and risks are set out in the offering documents.

Strategy 04

### Sovereign Debt & Quantitative Macro

Direct bilateral agreements and structured notes across G7 and allied economies, with a quantitative macro function that hedges the book and watches the spreads so the rest of the firm can sleep.

Instruments

Bilateral agreements, structured notes

Universe

G7 and allied emerging economies

Method

Quantitative macro, volatility hedging

Horizon

Short duration relative to the private book

Liquidity

Monthly to daily, by mandate

Run from

Zurich

#### What this book is for

It is the liquid counterweight to an illiquid firm. Sovereign exposure across G7 and allied economies, accessed directly and through structured notes, gives clients a part of their allocation that can move when the rest cannot.

#### How the quantitative function works

Models monitor sovereign spreads, volatility and cross-market relationships continuously and propose hedges and rebalancing. The partners in Zurich review and act. The models are tools of attention, not of conviction.

#### What we avoid

Leverage for its own sake, trades that depend on being right about an election, and anything we could not explain to a client in one paragraph. We would rather be a little late than loud.

#### What clients should expect

A more liquid, more frequently reported sleeve whose purpose is balance rather than brilliance. Sovereign and macro positions can lose money, and no outcome is assured; the offering documents describe the risks.

Partner letter · Q3

### On sovereign credit spread compression

Dear clients,

Spreads across European and G7 sovereign debt narrowed further this quarter, and for much of it the most profitable thing we could have done was to lean into that narrowing. We did not. Our quantitative function flagged the compression early and proposed modest hedges; the partners in Zurich accepted them and otherwise left the book alone.

This is not a letter about being right. It is a letter about the kind of wrong we are prepared to be. We would rather give up some of a rally than hold positions whose justification is that other people are holding them. The private book, meanwhile, did what it is supposed to do: it collected coupons, tested its covenants, and said nothing.

We will write again when there is something to say.

The Partners
Velour Capital, London and Zurich

Research

### Algorithmic risk mitigation in private debt

Private debt is illiquid, lightly priced and slow to admit bad news. That makes it an unusual place to apply quantitative risk tools, and an unusually good one.

This paper describes the volatility triggers and loan-to-value rebalancing protocols we run across our mandates: what they watch, how often, and what happens when one of them raises its hand. It also sets out their limits. A model cannot read a management team, and a trigger cannot tell you whether a covenant breach is a crisis or a conversation.

Our conclusion is unfashionable. The machines should be allowed to see everything and decide nothing. Their job is to make sure that the committee is never surprised, and never late.
