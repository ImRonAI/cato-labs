## Section #ethos: Three Tenets of Permanence
Index 03 / Structural Manifesto

###
Three Tenets of Permanence

Architecture begins where decoration ends. Our structures are engineered for multi-century lifespans across alpine and seismic terrains.

01 / Integrity

#### Material Honesty

We reject applied plaster, veneer, and cosmetic cladding. The load-bearing skeleton is the final interior and exterior surface. Concrete cures as an unedited record of gravity, hydration heat, and formwork pressure.

Unclad Cast Surface

02 / Permanence

#### Structural Monolithism

Buildings are geological interventions. By casting continuous monolithic volumes with post-tensioned cores, we eliminate fragile expansion joints and secondary framing, creating chambers that weather like bedrock.

Continuous Pour

03 / Reduction

#### Spatial Silence

True luxury is acoustic and visual quietude. High-density concrete absorbs urban and alpine wind turbulence, distilling the interior experience to shifting daylight, water reflection, and human proportion.

Acoustic Mass

### Haus am See — Lakeside Cantilever

Zürich, Switzerland · 47.3769° N, 8.5417° E

Conceived as a private study and acoustic chamber above Lake Zurich, Haus am See cantilevers 8.4 meters over a glacial moraine shelf. Morning light enters through a 240 mm linear roof slot, grazing the Douglas fir board-formed walls to mark solar time across the floor slab.

- **Structural System**: Post-tensioned box cantilever · 420 mm walls

- **Concrete Mix**: CEM III/B 42.5N · River Limmat washed aggregate

### The Beton Monolith

Zürich, Switzerland · 47.3769° N, 8.5417° E

Cast in twelve continuous lifts using rough-sawn Douglas fir planks harvested in Graubünden. High slag replacement (CEM III/B 42.5N) moderates hydration heat across 420 mm load-bearing perimeter walls, eliminating cold joints and surface crazing.

- **Coordinates**: 47.3769° N, 8.5417° E

- **Compressive Strength**: 45 MPa at 28 days (CEM III/B 42.5N)

- **Formwork**: Douglas fir board-formed, 120 mm tongue-and-groove

- **Water-Binder Ratio**: 0.41 w/b with polycarboxylate superplasticizer

### Kyoto Ravine Sanctuary

Kyōto, Japan · 35.0116° N, 135.7681° E

Spanning a damp mountain ravine outside Higashiyama, the sanctuary utilizes a 55 MPa silica-fume modified mix for ultra-low permeability against monsoon runoff. Japanese cedar (Sugi) formwork boards were lightly charred and wire-brushed prior to pouring, transferring a raised botanical grain onto the cured concrete.

- **Coordinates**: 35.0116° N, 135.7681° E

- **Compressive Strength**: 55 MPa · 8% Silica-fume replacement

- **Formwork**: Japanese cedar (Sugi) grain matrix

- **Seismic Isolation**: Lead-rubber base bearings recessed in bedrock
