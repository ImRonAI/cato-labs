Why high field

### Fusion power rises with the fourth power of magnetic field. Doubling the field lets a tokamak shrink to one-sixteenth of the volume for the same output. ReBCO tape makes that field practical for the first time.

#### Smaller machine

A compact core needs less steel, less concrete and less capital per megawatt. It can be built in a factory sequence rather than as a one-off civil project.

#### Known physics

The HG-400 is a conventional tokamak operated at high field. Confinement, heating and current drive follow the scaling laws established across decades of machines.

#### A grid asset

Continuous baseload, dispatchable heat and a footprint that fits an existing substation site. Fusion that behaves like infrastructure, not like an experiment.

## ReBCO toroidal field coils

The magnet is the reactor. Every dimension of the HG-400 follows from the field these coils can hold.

#### Why the tape matters

Rare-earth barium copper oxide is a ceramic superconductor deposited as a thin film on a steel ribbon. Unlike the niobium alloys of earlier machines it stays superconducting in fields above twenty tesla, and it does so at 20 kelvin rather than 4. That second fact is as important as the first: it allows conduction cooling by cryocoolers and removes liquid helium from the plant entirely.

The HG-400 coil set is designed to 22.4 tesla on axis at an operating current of 48.5 kiloamperes. Field strength enters fusion power as the fourth power, so this single number sets the machine's size. A conventional low-temperature superconducting tokamak needs roughly four times the linear dimension to reach the same output.

#### Holding twelve and a half gigajoules

The full coil set stores 12.5 GJ of magnetic energy. If any part of a coil loses superconductivity, that energy must be moved out of the winding before it heats a single spot beyond what the tape survives. The design requirement is five milliseconds from detection to dump; the protection system as designed acts in under 4.2 ms, using distributed voltage taps and fibre-optic temperature sensing along the winding.

#### What the Geneva line produces

The 2027 facility winds, impregnates and cold-tests complete D-shaped coils rather than sub-scale pancakes. Coil-to-coil reproducibility, measured as a current tolerance of ±0.02 % at operating point, is the acceptance criterion that qualifies a coil for the pilot plant.

## Lithium-lead breeding blanket

The blanket does two jobs the reactor cannot run without: it makes the fuel and it collects the heat.

#### Fuel that makes itself

Deuterium–tritium fusion releases most of its energy as a 14 MeV neutron. Tritium itself is scarce; a plant must breed what it burns. In the HG-400 the plasma is surrounded by a liquid eutectic of lithium and lead. Neutrons striking lithium produce tritium, and lead multiplies the neutron population so that slightly more tritium is made than consumed. The design tritium breeding ratio is 1.12, with the margin above unity covering losses in extraction, decay and start-up inventory for the next machine.

#### Heat at a useful temperature

The same liquid metal slows the neutrons and absorbs their energy, leaving the blanket as high-grade heat. Because it is a flowing liquid it also serves as its own coolant, carrying energy to the steam cycle without a separate breeder structure. For industrial offtakers this is the stream that can be delivered as process heat alongside electricity.

#### Materials and replacement

Blanket modules are designed to be exchanged through the vessel ports on a planned schedule, so neutron damage to structural steel is handled by maintenance rather than by lifetime limits on the whole machine.

- Tritium breeding ratio1.12

## Tungsten divertor

Everything the plasma rejects arrives on a strip a few centimetres wide. The divertor is where continuous operation is won or lost.

#### The exhaust problem

Open field lines at the plasma edge are steered to the bottom of the vessel, where helium ash and heat leave the confined region. In a compact high-field machine the power density on this strike surface is severe: the HG-400 design basis is 35 megawatts per square metre, roughly the heat flux on the surface of the sun.

#### Tungsten on micro-channels

Tungsten is chosen for its melting point and its low retention of tritium. Tiles are bonded to a heat sink threaded with sub-millimetre coolant channels, which keeps the conduction path short and the tile surface temperature well below the point at which tungsten recrystallises. The geometry is designed to be run continuously, not in pulses, which is what distinguishes a power plant from a research device.

#### Operating envelope

Strike-point sweeping and partial detachment, both routine on existing tokamaks, bring the time-averaged load within the material envelope. The 2026 prototype campaign validates the detachment control before the pilot plant divertor is frozen.

- ArmourTungsten tiles
- CoolingMicro-channel
- ModeContinuous, baseload
- Validation2026 prototype campaign

## 2026 — Prototype first plasma

Status: On schedule.

The prototype core at the Zurich facility is the first machine to combine Helios Grid's ReBCO coil set with a full plasma-facing wall. The campaign begins with low-power confinement trials: breakdown, current ramp, equilibrium control and divertor detachment, each measured against the design scaling.

First plasma retires the question of whether the magnet set performs at field with the plasma loads applied. It is deliberately not a fusion-power milestone; the point is to validate the coil, the control system and the diagnostics before anything larger is funded.

## Offtake for heavy industry

Electricity and high-grade heat from the same plant, for processes that cannot simply plug in.

#### Two products

The lithium-lead blanket removes fusion energy as high-grade heat before it reaches the turbine. For chemical, metals and materials producers that heat can be supplied directly, alongside electricity, from a plant on or near the works.

#### Why it matters

Industrial heat is among the hardest emissions to remove, because electrifying a furnace or a cracker is expensive and often impractical. A continuous heat source at process temperature changes that calculation.

#### Starting the conversation

Tell us the electrical load, the heat requirement and the site. Industrial agreements are structured individually around the process rather than from a standard block.

- **l**: Electricity and high-grade heat from the same plant, for processes that cannot simply plug in.
- **body**: Two products
The lithium-lead blanket removes fusion energy as high-grade heat before it reaches the turbine. For chemical, metals and materials producers that heat can be supplied directly, alongside electricity, from a plant on or near the works.
Why it matters
Industrial heat is among the hardest emissions to remove, because electrifying a furnace or a cracker is expensive and often impractical. A continuous heat source at process temperature changes that calculation.
Starting the conversation
Tell us the electrical load, the heat requirement and the site. Industrial agreements are structured individually around the process rather than from a standard block.
