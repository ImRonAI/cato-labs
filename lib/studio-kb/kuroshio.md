## Section #story: A room built to hold silence
01 間

### A room built to hold silence

- CounterOne slab of hinokiKiso valley cypress, around two hundred years old. Cut once, never stained.

- WallsYakisugi cedarCharred to black. It swallows sound; the room stays quiet even when it is full.

- JoineryNo metalTraditional timber joints throughout. Nothing in the room was nailed.

- SeatsTwelveAll at the counter. There is no table in the room.

Ginza is loud. The counter is not. Twelve guests sit along one piece of wood and watch the same pair of hands for two and a half hours.

Ma — the pause — is the discipline here. The fish is bought that morning. The rice is seasoned with red vinegar and served warm. Each piece arrives when it is ready and not a moment sooner, and the chef says little, because there is little that needs saying.

The name is the current: the Kuroshio, the warm black stream that carries bluefin north along the Pacific coast of Japan. Almost everything on the counter was, at some point, in it.

The room

### The room

The counter is a single slab of hinoki cypress from the Kiso valley, in Nagano, from a tree that was around two hundred years old when it was felled. It was planed once and has never been stained, oiled or sealed. It is wiped with a damp cloth after each seating and nothing else. Over time it has taken on a faint gold where the hands rest.

#### Walls

The walls are yakisugi — cedar charred black on the surface, then brushed. Charring preserves the wood without chemicals and gives the room a surface that absorbs sound rather than returning it. Twelve people at a counter would normally be loud. They are not.

#### Joinery

Every joint in the room is a timber joint, cut by hand. There is no metal fixing anywhere: no nails, no screws, no brackets. The joiners worked in the traditional manner, and the room can in principle be taken apart and reassembled.

#### Light

There is very little of it. A line of recessed lamps runs the length of the counter and lights the wood, the plate and the chef's hands. The guests sit just outside it.

#### Seats

Twelve, all at the counter, all facing the same way. There is no table and no private room. Everyone sees the same thing.

Policies

### Policies

#### Seatings

Two a night, at 17:30 and 20:30 Japan Standard Time, Tuesday to Saturday. Each seating is served as one counter; the first course goes to everyone at once, so please arrive ten minutes before.

#### Party size

Requests are taken for one to four guests. The whole counter — twelve seats — can be reserved by a single party at either seating. Groups of five to eleven are split across the two seatings or two evenings.

#### Cancellation

Forty-eight hours' notice. The fish is bought at auction for the number of guests confirmed, and cannot be returned. Changes inside forty-eight hours are treated as a cancellation.

#### Allergies and preferences

Tell us when you request, not on the night. Raw shellfish and specific fish can be omitted. The progression cannot be rebuilt as a vegetarian or vegan menu.

#### Children

Guests of twelve and over are welcome at the counter.

#### Photographs

Of the food, yes; please be brief. Of the chef and other guests, please ask first. No flash.

#### Confirmation

A request is not a booking. The head concierge confirms personally. If the evening you asked for is taken, another will be offered.

Privacy

### Privacy

Kuroshio Ginza collects the details you give in a reservation request — your name, how to reach you, the date and size of your party, and any dietary notes — and uses them only to confirm and prepare your seating.

Dietary and allergy information is shared with the chef and kitchen, and with no one else. We do not sell, rent or share guest details, and we do not use them for marketing unless you ask us to.

This page is a design demonstration. The reservation form does not transmit anything; the details you enter stay in your browser and are discarded when you leave.

If you would like a record of your request removed, write to the counter and it will be done.

### room
- **crumb**: The room
- **html**: The room

 Walls
The walls are yakisugi — cedar charred black on the surface, then brushed. Charring preserves the wood without chemicals and gives the room a surface that absorbs sound rather than returning it. Twelve people at a counter would normally be loud. They are not.

 Joinery
Every joint in the room is a timber joint, cut by hand. There is no metal fixing anywhere: no nails, no screws, no brackets. The joiners worked in the traditional manner, and the room can in principle be taken apart and reassembled.

 Light
There is very little of it. A line of recessed lamps runs the length of the counter and lights the wood, the plate and the chef's hands. The guests sit just outside it.

 Seats
Twelve, all at the counter, all facing the same way. There is no table and no private room. Everyone sees the same thing.
